# Digital Distortion Editorial
Status: candidate; grounded in [GD-0008](../cases/GD-0008.md), transfer NOT_RUN.
## Distinctive design logic
Use selective loss of visual information as an expressive contrast to stable typography. Unlike a generic glitch overlay, retain a recognizable anchor and transform a secondary region into a directional texture that also serves composition. Fractal haze is one observed variant; do not require mathematical fractals, monochrome, or horizontal stripes for every brief.
## Design decisions
Choose the protected feature by communication goal (face, product silhouette, logo or material). Choose distortion direction to reinforce the intended movement or contrast the subject geometry. Establish a quiet information zone before effects. Vary band lengths and intensity to avoid mechanical repetition. Keep semantic text crisp and independently editable. Use grain and blur on separate layers. A neutral tonal palette can emphasize edge quality; brand color is equally valid when it supports hierarchy.
## Production options
Layer masks plus directional blur, sliced displacement, or procedural shader are alternatives. Start with the simplest controllable static method. Use motion only when justified and test reduced motion and performance. Do not claim any method is the source creator's technique.
## Retrieval cues
Experimental fashion, fractal haze, scanline smear, directional distortion, monochrome editorial, digital texture, motion-like still, wide geometric type. Combine with fashion editorial, industrial editorial or experimental typography when brief warrants it. Avoid if distortion compromises product fidelity or required information.
## Evidence and QA
GD-0008 demonstrates an observed composition, not validated production capability. Test recognition, type contrast, effect restraint, original asset rights, crop and output medium before promotion.
